# Pocket Tracker — Learn to Make Music

A hands-on course for someone who has never made music before. Every lesson uses only what's in the app: the three tracks (**Bass · Lead · Drum**), the 16-step grid, the scale helper, the drum pads, and the synth.

**How the grid works, in one breath:** each pattern is 16 rows tall. Playback runs top to bottom on a loop. Each row is one "step" — a sixteenth of the bar. The four amber-numbered rows (00, 04, 08, 0C) are the **main beats** — that's where your foot taps. Everything else falls between the beats.

**The one habit to build first:** *tap the cell, then tap the sound.* Select where it goes, then choose what goes there. Auto-advance moves you down a row so you can rattle notes in. Undo is free — mess around fearlessly.

---

## Lesson 0 — Press play and listen (5 min)

Goal: understand the loop before touching anything.

1. Open the app. There's already a demo pattern loaded.
2. Press the big green **play** button. Listen. Press it again to stop.
3. Watch the highlighted row travel down the grid. *That moving row is "now."* When it wraps back to the top, the loop repeats.
4. Change the **Tempo** up and down. Notice fast vs. slow. Put it back around 120.

**You learned:** music here is a 16-step loop, and tempo is how fast the loop spins.

---

## Lesson 1 — Your first beat: kick and snare (15 min)

The drum track is the skeleton. Almost every song in pop, hip-hop, rock, and dance is built on one idea: **kick on 1, snare on 3.**

1. Go to the **Song** tab and press **Clear all** to start empty. (Undo brings the demo back if you want it.)
2. Tap the **Drum** cell on row **00**. In the drawer, tap **KICK**.
3. Tap the **Drum** cell on row **08**. Tap **SNARE**.
4. Press play. That "boom … clap …" is the heartbeat of most music you know.

Now make it drive:

5. Add a **KICK** on row **04** as well (so kicks are on 00 and 04, snare on 08).
6. Try a kick on **0C** too. Play. Feel how more kicks = more push.

**Challenge:** put a **SNARE** on row **0C** instead of a kick. Boom-boom-clap-clap. Different feel, same skeleton.

**You learned:** the kick is the low pulse, the snare is the backbeat. Their placement *is* the groove.

---

## Lesson 2 — Hi-hats: filling the space (15 min)

The hi-hat is the "tick-tick-tick" that fills the gaps and makes a beat feel busy and alive.

1. Keep your kick+snare from Lesson 1.
2. Put a **HI-HAT** on every *even* row that's empty: 02, 06, 0A, 0E. Play. Already more motion.
3. Now go further — a hat on **every** empty row (01, 03, 05...). This is a "16th-note hat." Common in trap and pop.

**Here's where combos matter.** On row 00 you have a kick *and* you'd like a hat ticking there too — but one cell holds one thing. That's what the **KICK+HAT** and **SNARE+HAT** pads are for:

4. Change row **00** from KICK to **KICK+HAT**. Change row **08** from SNARE to **SNARE+HAT**.
5. Now your hat ticks *through* the beats instead of stopping for them. Play. That's a real, full drum groove.

**You learned:** hats control energy and density. Combos let the hat keep going while the kick or snare also hits — that's how real beats are layered.

## Lesson 3 — Meet the scale (10 min, do before any melody)

Before writing a single note, turn on the tool that makes notes sound *right*.

1. Go to the **Keys** tab (or select a Bass/Lead cell so the keyboard appears in the drawer).
2. Find the **Key** and **Scale** pickers. Leave Key on **C**, Scale on **Major (happy)**.
3. Look at the keyboard: some keys are bright, some are dimmed. **The bright keys are "in the key" — they sound at home together.** The dimmed ones can clash.
4. The green-outlined key with a dot is the **root** — the "home base" note. In C Major that's C.

Rule of thumb for now: **stay on bright keys and you can't really go wrong.** Later you'll break that on purpose.

> **Beginner shortcut:** switch Scale to **Minor Pentatonic**. Only five keys stay lit, and *any* order of them sounds good. It's the training-wheels scale — great for a first melody.

**You learned:** a scale is a small set of notes that belong together. The app shows you which ones. Home base is the root.

## Lesson 4 — Your first bassline (20 min)

The bass connects the drums to the melody. It's low, simple, and usually locks to the kick.

1. Pick a bass sound: **Synth** tab → target **Bass** → try the **808 Sub Bass** or **Synth Bass** preset. Press **Load**.
2. Back on **Song**, the golden rule of beginner bass: **put a bass note wherever the kick hits.** Your kick is on 00 and 04 — select those Bass cells.
3. For your first bassline, play the **root** on both. In C Major, that's **C** (use a low octave — hit **Lower** so you're around C3). Select Bass row 00 → tap **C**. Row 04 → tap **C**.
4. Play. The bass and kick now move as one. That "locked" feeling is the foundation of groove.

Make it musical:

5. Change the row 04 bass note to a different **bright** key — try **G** or **A**. Two-note basslines carry a lot of songs.
6. Add bass notes on rows 08 and 0C too. Keep them on bright keys. A four-note bassline (C – G – A – F is a classic) already sounds like a song.

**Watch the grid:** if a bass note shows a small **amber dot**, that note is *off-key* for your current scale. Not forbidden — but if something sounds wrong, that dot is the first place to look.

**You learned:** bass locks to the kick and lives on the low, in-key notes. Root notes are the safe, strong choice.

## Lesson 5 — Your first melody (25 min)

The lead is the part people hum. It's higher than the bass and has more movement.

1. **Synth** tab → target **Lead** → load a clear sound like **Square Lead**, **Sawtooth Lead**, or **8-Bit Chiptune**.
2. Melodies breathe — they are **not** on every step. Leave gaps. Silence is part of the tune.
3. Start simple: put lead notes only on the main beats (00, 04, 08, 0C), all on **bright** keys, around octave 4 or 5 (use **Higher**).
4. Play with the drums and bass under it. Congratulations — that's a complete piece of music.

Make it sing:

5. **Steps, not leaps.** Moving to a neighboring bright key (C→D→E) sounds smooth; big jumps sound dramatic. Mix mostly steps with the occasional jump.
6. **Start and end on home.** Try ending your 16 steps landing back on the root (C). Notice how "resolved" it feels — like a sentence ending in a period.
7. Add a couple of off-beat notes (odd rows) for surprise. Use the **Note off** button (the drawer's "hold release") to cut a long note short where you want a gap.

**You learned:** melody = movement + space. Steps are smooth, jumps are bold, and returning to the root feels like home.

---

## Lesson 6 — Shape the sound (20 min)

Same notes, totally different vibe — that's the synth's job. Work on the **Lead** so you can hear it clearly.

Play your loop, then move **one** slider at a time and *listen*:

- **Waveform** — the raw tone. *Sine* = soft/pure, *Square* = hollow/retro, *Saw* = bright/buzzy, *Triangle* = mellow. This is the biggest change.
- **Attack** — how fast the note arrives. Low = instant/plucky. High = slow swell (turn it up for a dreamy pad).
- **Release** — how long it rings after. Short = tight. Long = smeary tails.
- **Cutoff (brightness)** — dark and muffled on the left, bright and open on the right. Sweep it while playing; this is *the* classic synth move.
- **Wobble (LFO)** — adds motion. Small depth = gentle shimmer. Big depth + medium rate = the dubstep "wob."

Then:

- Turn on **Echo (Delay)** and raise **Mix**. Great on a sparse lead — it fills space with repeats.
- Compare loading a preset (e.g. **Dream Pad** vs **Acid Bass**) and reading the sliders it set. Presets are recipes; now you can read the recipe.

**You learned:** a note's *character* comes from waveform + envelope + filter. Small moves = big changes.

## Lesson 7 — Timing and feel (15 min)

1. Turn on **Click** (the metronome). Now every beat ticks. Play and *tap your foot* with the downbeat (the louder tick on row 00).
2. Turn on **Count-in**. Press play — you get "4, 3, 2, 1" before it starts. Use this when you want to be ready to hear or perform along.
3. Experiment with **Tempo**: the same pattern at 90 feels lazy, at 140 feels urgent. Tempo is an instrument too.

**You learned:** the beat is a grid you can feel. The metronome trains your internal clock.

---

## Lesson 8 — Build a song from patterns (25 min)

One 16-step loop is a *section*. Songs are sections in sequence.

1. You have **4 patterns** (the Pattern button cycles 1→2→3→4).
2. Make **Pattern 1** your main groove (drums + bass + lead).
3. Switch to **Pattern 2**. Build a *variation*: same bass, but a different melody, or a busier drum fill. Tip: **Copy pattern** in Pattern 1, paste into Pattern 2, then change just a few cells.
4. Make **Pattern 3** a "breakdown" — drop the drums, leave bass + lead, so it feels quieter.
5. Set the loop mode to **All patterns** (the Loop button). Press play — it now plays 1→2→3→4 and repeats. That arc (groove → variation → breakdown → back) is song structure.
6. **Save** when you like it.

**You learned:** copy-vary-arrange. Contrast between sections is what makes a song go somewhere.

## Quick reference — beginner "rules" worth knowing

These aren't laws. They're safe starting points you'll enjoy breaking later.

- **Kick = pulse, snare = backbeat, hat = energy.** Kick on 00/04, snare on 08 is home base.
- **Bass follows the kick** and lives on low, in-key notes — root notes are safest.
- **Melody needs space.** Don't fill every step. Steps sound smooth, leaps sound bold.
- **End on the root** to feel resolved.
- **Stay on the bright (in-key) keys** until you can hear why an off-key note works.
- **An amber dot in the grid** = that note is outside your key. A clue, not an error.
- **Change one thing at a time** when shaping sound, so you learn what each control does.
- **Undo is free.** Experiment like nothing can break — because nothing can.

## A 20-minute "make something now" recipe

For when you just want a track fast:

1. Clear all. Key = C, Scale = Major (or Minor Pentatonic for safety).
2. Drums: KICK+HAT on 00 and 04, SNARE+HAT on 08, HAT on the empty even rows.
3. Bass: root note (C, low octave) on 00 and 04; try G on 08 and F on 0C.
4. Lead: 4–6 notes on bright keys, mostly on beats, ending on C.
5. Pick a Lead preset you like; nudge Cutoff and Release to taste.
6. Turn on Click, set tempo to feel, press play. Save. Done.
